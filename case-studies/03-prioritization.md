# Prioritizing product development under startup constraints

**Status:** proposed retrospective framework. Historical priorities and scores must be reconstructed from authorized records before being described as past decisions.

## Competing needs

GrapeRoot had to balance developer experience, adoption, enterprise requirements, engineering effort, revenue potential, and founder capacity. A useful portfolio artifact should show the trade-offs rather than produce a fictional “correct” ranking.

## Candidate opportunities

| Opportunity | User or business value | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reduce first-session setup friction | More developers reach first value | Whether friction is the main activation constraint |
| Improve context quality for cross-file tasks | Better task outcomes and trust | Whether quality gains survive different repositories |
| Add enterprise access and licensing controls | Enable team adoption and buying | Whether enough qualified demand exists |
| Improve cost and usage reporting | Make value visible to users and buyers | Whether reporting changes retention or conversion |
| Expand distribution and community loops | More qualified product exposure | Whether reach converts to repeat usage |

## How I would prioritize

Score reach, impact, confidence, and effort only after defining each scale. Then review the result against strategic fit, sequencing dependencies, qualitative customer evidence, and risk. Record the rationale for overrides. Do not imply these scores were used historically until you find the source decision.

## Now / Next / Later (proposed)

**Now:** remove the largest activation friction and define task-quality and cost measurement.

**Next:** run an authorized enterprise pilot with explicit requirements and a review decision.

**Later:** invest in scalable controls, packaging, and integrations only if demand and pilot evidence justify the cost.

## Decision log template

For each real decision, record the date, problem, evidence available, options considered, chosen path, owner, trade-offs, expected outcome, review date, actual outcome, and what changed afterward. This is more useful in interviews than an unexplained prioritization score.
